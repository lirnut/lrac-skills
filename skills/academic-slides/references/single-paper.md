# Single-paper talks

## Set the explanatory depth

- Establish the paper's actual question, contribution, and relation to prior work. Tailor background to the audience rather than repeating a field introduction by default.
- Inspect the exact full-text version and relevant supplements. Use a one-paper brief with the selection fields from the global workflow; do not invent a multi-paper selection exercise.

## Explain the evidence chain

- Organize the core explanation as problem → approach → results → interpretation and limits, adapting the order to the paper rather than imposing fixed slide counts.
- Explain the task, model or participants, manipulations, controls, and metrics before interpreting plots. Show essential update or reasoning processes when needed to understand the results.
- Use the smallest sufficient chain of original figures and tables. Distinguish an illustrative trajectory from aggregate evidence and main findings from ablations or boundary tests.
- Explain why a result supports the authors' claim and what remains untested. Keep architectural assumptions separate from demonstrated effects.

## Close within scope

- Summarize the paper's supported contribution and limitations; separate presenter criticism from the authors' interpretation.
- Do not require a field-wide research gap, a personal proposal, or a speculative extension unless requested.
- Apply the global evidence, communication, and artifact gates; this mode changes explanation depth, not evidence standards.
