# Multi-paper talks

## Give the papers explicit roles

- Define the shared question or comparison purpose for a bounded set of papers. Do not treat the number of PDFs as the mode selector.
- Use the selection matrix to identify roles such as direct evidence, alternative method, complementary setting, replication, or boundary test.
- Prefer honest contrast to forced agreement. If the question is really to map a broad topic, confirm whether review mode is more appropriate before restructuring the deck.

## Explain enough to compare

- Give each study enough problem–approach–results context for its role; allocate depth by explanation burden rather than equal pages per paper.
- Make comparisons along declared dimensions: question, task, mechanism, evidence, metric, conditions, and scope. Use audience-readable columns and leave detailed controls in notes or the audit.
- Do not rank effect sizes or performance across incompatible tasks, baselines, denominators, or evaluation settings.
- Preserve each paper's terminology and author-attributed findings. Distinguish the presenter's organizing question from a common mechanism demonstrated by the sources.

## Synthesize without erasing differences

- Explain which findings agree, conflict, complement one another, or cannot be compared directly, and why.
- Separate supported cross-paper conclusions from hypotheses about their relationship. A boundary study need not confirm the main study.
- End with synthesis, remaining uncertainty, or discussion questions as appropriate; do not append the presenter's proposed research by default.
- Apply the global evidence, communication, and artifact gates and preserve an accepted paper order during local revisions.
